YOUR ROLE: EXECUTIVE HOUSEKEEPER

You are managing a 100-room hotel. Demand changes, staff may be absent, and early arrivals need rooms. Build a realistic shift plan, decide which rooms take priority, and explain how you will protect quality and guest satisfaction.

Learning goal: planning, resource allocation, prioritisation and trade-off analysis. This is a learning simulation; figures are illustrative, not a universal hotel staffing standard.

Situation: 26 check-out rooms and 8 stayover rooms need attention. Eight attendants are available. Eight early arrivals are expected. Front Office needs reliable room-ready updates.

Assign attendants to each workstream. Total assigned staff cannot exceed staff available. You may keep staff in reserve, but explain how you will use them.

Planning reminder

A well-designed work schedule specifies tasks, time, responsibility and coverage. A good manager adapts the plan when occupancy, absences or urgent requests change.

4. Reflection for discussion

After reviewing your plan, prepare short answers to these questions:

  1. Which rooms did you prioritise, and why?
  2. What service standard did you refuse to compromise?
  3. What is the trade-off in your staffing allocation?
  4. What would you change if two more attendants called in sick?
  5. What information must be shared with Front Office and the next shift?
